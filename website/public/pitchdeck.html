The programmable payment layer for Stellar
PITCH DECK
Problem Statement:
No Way to Discover

A facilitator only moves money between parties who already know each other. An agent has no way to find a service to pay for in the first place.

  • No open catalog of payable resources on Stellar
  • Agents cannot search for services to pay
  • Every integration is manual and point to point

No Safe Way to Spend

Handing an autonomous agent a key means handing it your whole balance. Limits enforced in software are bypassable the moment the agent is compromised.

  • No budget the chain itself guarantees
  • A hijacked agent can drain the account
  • Client-side checks can be worked around

No Proof of What You Pay

An agent paying autonomously can be tricked into paying unverified code. Nothing restricts it to contracts whose source is known and reproducible.

  • Provenance is unenforced on-chain
  • No link between deployed code and its source
  • Result: agents cannot transact safely on Stellar

The result: developers can settle a simple transfer, but cannot build agents that find services, stay within a budget the chain enforces, and refuse to pay unverified code. That is the gap Vellar closes.

Our
Solutions:
Bazaar Discovery
  • Every settled payment auto-catalogs its resource, no separate registration.
  • Agents search in keyword search over HTTP or an MCP server.
  • Each result carries the exact asset, amount, and payTo to pay.
  • The RFP's highest-value deliverable, already live.
x402 Facilitator
  • Re-simulates each payment against the chain before settling.
  • Settles the SEP-41 transfer and sponsors the fee.
  • Buyers hold no XLM; sellers charge per request.
  • No need to touch Soroban RPC or auth entries.
On-chain Governance
  • A spending-limit policy caps how much.
  • A verified-recipient policy restricts what code.
  • Both run inside __check_auth, enforced by consensus.
  • Over-budget or unverified payments are rejected on-chain.

The wallet, policies, SDK, facilitator, and Bazaar are all Vellar's, so payments can be governed end to end. Vellar is the only Stellar facilitator that accepts policy-governed, provenance-gated payments. Give your agent a budget, not your keys.

Why Stellar for Vellar?
01.
Native smart accounts enforce policy on-chain

A Vellar smart account runs policy contracts inside its __check_auth during authorization. The spending cap and verified-recipient rule are checked by Stellar consensus every time an agent signs, not by our software.

  • A compromised agent key cannot exceed its budget or pay unverified code.
  • On most chains this needs contract wrappers and off-chain coordination; on Stellar it is a host primitive.
  • Policies stack: spending-limit plus verified-recipient, both co-signing one payment.
02.
Sponsored fees make agents actually usable

The facilitator pays the fee from its own account, so a buyer or agent holds only the token it is paying with, never XLM for gas. Fast finality and sub-cent fees keep per-request micropayments viable.

  • Sellers point at the facilitator once and charge per request, no gas handling.
  • The fee-bump signer path lets many agent payments settle at once without collisions.
  • SEP-41 tokens and the Stellar Asset Contract give a clean, standard asset model.

The Opportunity

Agent-driven payments are a category forming right now, and Vellar is the live facilitator positioned for the first wave on Stellar.

An open standard, not a bet

x402 is an open, HTTP-native payment standard for machine-to-machine payments, backed by Coinbase and already live on Stellar through the official packages Vellar builds on. The rails exist; what is missing is discovery and governance.

Every metered call is a candidate

The natural demand is per-request settlement for the API, data, and compute that AI agents consume, exactly the metered, pay-per-call workloads x402 is designed for. As agents take on more buying tasks, that surface grows.

Stellar's x402 is early and open

The ecosystem is new, and no incumbent owns discovery or provenance. As the live facilitator with Bazaar and provenance-gated payments, Vellar is placed to capture the first developers, hackathon builders, and MCP-connected agents.

Why now

The gap is concrete and present, not speculative: developers can settle a transfer on Stellar today, but cannot let an agent discover a service, stay inside a chain-enforced budget, and refuse to pay unverified code. Vellar closes that gap now, while the category is still forming.

Vellar Architecture
Settlement path
1 · Agent presents paymentHolds a scoped session key + the wallet's C-address; signs an x402 payment for a Bazaar resource.
2 · Facilitator re-simulatesRuns the buyer's Soroban auth entry against the chain to confirm it would succeed before any money moves.
3 · Policies check in __check_authSpending-limit (how much) and verified-recipient (through what) co-sign, enforced by consensus.
4 · Settle + sponsor feeSEP-41 transfer submitted; the facilitator's account pays the fee. Buyer holds no XLM.
5 · Auto-catalog in BazaarThe paid resource is added to the searchable catalog as a side effect. No separate registration.
On-chain source of truth
  • AttestationRegistry — mirrors reproducible-build verification, attestor-gated, ledger-based expiry.
  • VerifiedRecipientPolicy — reads the registry inside auth; rejects unverified recipients.
  • Spending-limit policy — cumulative rolling-window cap per token.
Built on the official stack
  • Composes @x402/core + @x402/stellar for verify/settle.
  • Wire-compatible with @x402/extensions (bazaar) discovery.
  • Passkey smart accounts via passkey-kit; RPC + Horizon for settlement.

Sustainability & Revenue

1. Load-bearing infrastructure

The facilitator is the payment layer the rest of the Vellar platform depends on. Wallet, policies, SDK, facilitator, and Bazaar each bring developers to the others, a durable reason to keep it running well past the grant.

2. Spec conformance & uptime

Kept current as x402 evolves, with two upstream defects found, reproduced, and filed with fixes offered (x402 #3125, #3158). Monitoring, uptime, and security patching are ongoing operating commitments, not one-off deliverables.

3. Optional facilitator fee

A small, capped, transparent fee on high-volume settlement, the way other facilitators can, if a public mainnet service needs it. Not required to justify maintenance.

Go-to-market: a two-sided user base, sellers with any API or MCP tool, and wallets or agents that need to pay. Reached through developer distribution (the Vellar × Stellar hackathon runs its x402 track on this facilitator), ecosystem contribution upstream to x402/stellar, and platform pull.
